How to read an idea

The deal sheet runs from the purchase to the sale. Each line is a delivery term and what it adds. FOB is on board at the load port. CFR adds the freight, CIF the cargo insurance, DAP what is lost on the way, and duty paid the import duties. The cargo stops at the term it is sold on. Whatever comes after is the buyer's cost and stays out of the margin. Financing and inspection stay with the seller on every term.

A spot cargo costs what its next best outlet pays. The US LNG idea buys the cargo at its European netback, not at Henry Hub plus the tolling fee. That cost-plus price is only open to a long-term offtaker, who earns it whichever way the ship sails. The idea earns the gap between Asia and Europe.

Per day divides the margin by the days the cargo is tied up. That is the voyage for a cargo and the time to the buy-back for a storage trade. It compares a short trade with a long one on the same product. It is not annualised, because a desk cannot repeat the same cargo back to back all year.

One day late is the ship's hire plus the interest on the cargo for one more day. Demurrage is usually claimed back from whoever caused the delay, so this is the exposure and not a forecast.

If it works, if it goes wrong. Both cases move today's margin by the amount written with the idea, so they follow the market.
