Where should a Richards Bay cargo go

South Africa swings between Europe and Asia. Europe pays API2, a CIF price. In Japan the cargo competes with Newcastle coal, so it is worth NEWC plus the Newcastle → Japan freight, a CFR price. Each bar is that delivered value, split into what is left at Richards Bay and what the voyage costs. The Rotterdam leg includes EU allowances. The arb is open when the netback beats API4. API2 and NEWC are taken for the month the cargo arrives.

An Indian cement or power buyer compares fuels per million kcal, landed and duty paid. Duty is 2.75% on coal and 11% on petcoke, charged on the CIF value. Does the premium pay for the trip

A tonne in an LME warehouse in Rotterdam, shipped to the US or Japan. The diamond is what the destination pays over LME. For copper that is COMEX − LME, for aluminium the regional premium. The bars are what it costs to get there, starting with the Rotterdam premium the metal would fetch if sold where it sits. When the bars run past the diamond, the trade does not work. The US tariff is charged on the metal value, not on the premium.

Method and limits

Delivery terms. Every index comes with a term, shown in the prices table. API2 is CIF, so the seller insures the cargo. Iron ore and scrap are CFR, so the buyer does. India is compared duty paid, with a 0.3% handling loss on the way. Coil is bought ex-works and sold duty paid. Costs past the sale term are the buyer's.

Iron ore. Netback = 62% CFR China − freight per dry tonne − curve. Port Hedland → Qingdao is Baltic C5 and Tubarão → Qingdao is C3, both on a 170 kt Capesize. Grade differences, such as 65% Brazilian ore, are ignored.

Thermal coal. Richards Bay → Rotterdam on a 150 kt Capesize, paid at API2. Richards Bay → Japan competes with Newcastle coal, so the delivered value is NEWC plus the Panamax freight from Newcastle to Kashima. All three indices are 6,000 kcal NAR, so there is no calorific adjustment.

India fuel. Landed cost per million kcal. South African coal is API4 at 6,000 NAR, Indonesian 4,200 GAR is taken as 3,800 NAR, and US Gulf petcoke 6.5% S as 7,900. Sulphur, ash and plant constraints are not priced, which matters for petcoke.

Coking coal. SGX TSI premium hard coking coal FOB Australia plus freight and insurance. Mid-vol and US high-vol coals are not covered.

Carbon and power. A tonne of 6,000 kcal coal holds 6.978 MWh of heat. Coal emits 0.341 t CO2 per MWh of heat and gas 0.202. Switching EUA = (gas fuel cost − coal fuel cost) / (coal CO2 − gas CO2), all per MWh of power.

Scrap and steel. Scrap freight is a Supramax trip. Coil freight is a flat breakbulk assumption. US coil futures are per short ton and converted. EU duties use the "all other companies" rate of Regulations 2023/1122 and 2023/1123, while named Chinese mills pay 18.1% to 35.9%. CBAM certificates = default emissions × 1.10 − 0.975 × benchmark, from Implementing Regulation 2025/2621, priced at the EUA future as a proxy for the quarterly certificate price.

Metals. Margin = destination premium − Rotterdam duty-unpaid premium (aluminium) − load-out − freight − insurance − tariff − financing. The Rotterdam premium is the CME duty-unpaid premium future. Freight and tariffs are assumptions you can override in the freight file.

Limits. Front-month futures, delayed. No quality premiums, no LME queue or warrant costs, no letters of credit. A handling loss and port costs are assumptions, not quotes.
