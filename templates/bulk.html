Timing. A cargo bought today loads in about 30 days. The purchase is priced for the loading day and the sale for the landing day, both read off the forward curve. A monthly index is pinned to the 15th of its month and a future to its expiry, and days in between are interpolated. Nothing steps when a front contract expires.

Delivery terms. Every index comes with a term, shown in the prices table. API2 is CIF, so the seller insures the cargo. Iron ore and scrap are CFR, so the buyer does. India is compared duty paid, with a 0.3% handling loss on the way. Coil is bought ex-works and sold duty paid. Costs past the sale term are the buyer's.

Iron ore. Netback = 62% CFR China − freight per dry tonne − curve. Port Hedland → Qingdao is Baltic C5 and Tubarão → Qingdao is C3, both on a 170 kt Capesize. Freight is paid on wet tonnes and converted to dry tonnes with the moisture assumption. The buyer insures the cargo. Grade differences, such as 65% Brazilian ore, are ignored.

Thermal coal. Richards Bay → Rotterdam on a 150 kt Capesize, paid at API2. Richards Bay → Japan competes with Newcastle coal, so the delivered value is NEWC plus the Panamax freight from Newcastle to Kashima, less 5 $/t. That discount is an assumption for a buyer whose boilers are tuned to Australian coal. All three indices are 6,000 kcal NAR, so there is no calorific adjustment. The Rotterdam leg includes EU allowances.

India fuel. Landed cost per million kcal. Duty is 2.75% on coal and 11% on petcoke, charged on the CIF value. A fuel's margin is how much it could rise before the next cheapest one takes the buyer. Petcoke has no public futures, so its price is typed in by hand and flagged when it gets old. South African coal is API4 at 6,000 NAR, Indonesian 4,200 GAR is taken as 3,800 NAR, and US Gulf petcoke 6.5% S as 7,900. Sulphur, ash and plant constraints are not priced, which matters for petcoke. All three move on a Supramax trip, where the charterer pays the days on hire and not the way back. The South African trip adds the ballast bonus owners ask to reach Richards Bay. The Indonesian ship is delivered around Singapore, so the run down to Kalimantan is on hire.

Coking coal. SGX TSI premium hard coking coal FOB Australia plus freight and insurance. Mid-vol and US high-vol coals are not covered.

Carbon and power. A tonne of 6,000 kcal coal holds 6.978 MWh of heat. Coal emits 0.341 t CO2 per MWh of heat and gas 0.202. Switching EUA = (gas fuel cost − coal fuel cost) / (coal CO2 − gas CO2), all per MWh of power. The four prices are for the first full month ahead.

Scrap and steel. Scrap freight is a Supramax trip. A route with no fixture of its own takes the hire of a trip leaving the same coast. Coil freight is a flat breakbulk assumption. Both coil indices are ex-works. European coil pays about 30 $/t to reach a ship in Antwerp, and an import sells about 70 $/t under domestic coil with port costs. Both figures are assumptions. US coil futures are per short ton and converted. EU duties use the "all other companies" rate of Regulations 2023/1122 and 2023/1123, while named Chinese mills pay 18.1% to 35.9%. CBAM certificates = default emissions × 1.10 − 0.975 × benchmark, from Implementing Regulation 2025/2621, priced at the EUA future as a proxy for the quarterly certificate price{% for r in routes if r.route_id == 'HRC_CN_EU_QUOTA' and r.cbam %}. Today that is {{ r.cbam_certs|num(2) }} t CO2 per tonne of Chinese coil, or {{ r.cbam|num(0) }} $/t{% endfor %}.

Metals. Margin = destination premium − Rotterdam duty-unpaid premium (aluminium) − load-out − freight − insurance − tariff − financing. The Rotterdam premium is the CME duty-unpaid premium future. Freight and tariffs are assumptions you can override in the freight file. The US tariff is charged on the metal value, not on the premium. The metal is paid at the LME price of the day it loads and sold at the premium of the day it lands, which matters when a premium curve falls as fast as the Midwest one.

Limits. Settlement prices, a day old. Steel and scrap contracts on the LME are thin and their curves are close to flat. No quality premiums, no LME queue or warrant costs, no letters of credit. A handling loss and port costs are assumptions, not quotes.
